# Proposed AUSLAN Sign Vocabulary — Australian Raptors

**Status:** Provisional. Submitted for review by AUSLAN-fluent
consultants and Deaf community representatives.
**Author:** Brian Fernández Báez, Computer Systems Engineer
(independent researcher; prospective Master's by Research / MPhil
applicant in Australia, with a future PhD pathway).
**Project:** Australian Raptor CNN + AUSLAN — research prototype.

---

## Design rationale

Each proposed sign was derived from a **diagnostic flight or
behaviour pattern** unique to the species, following two design
principles standard in scientific sign vocabularies (Lang & Steely,
2003; Quinto-Pozos & Reynolds, 2012):

1. **Iconicity:** the motion of the sign should resemble the
   characteristic flight / behaviour pattern.
2. **Phonological economy:** signs should reuse hand shapes,
   locations and movements already part of the AUSLAN inventory
   wherever possible, to ease learning and minimise cognitive load.

The descriptions below specify five parameters per sign:
**hand shape · location · palm orientation · movement · non-manual
component**. These are the standard parameters used by Auslan
Signbank and by the Trevor Johnston (Macquarie University) corpus.

---

## 1. Wedge-tailed Eagle (*Aquila audax*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Both hands in **flat-B** (open hand, fingers extended). |
| Location | Both hands meet at chest height, palms facing the body. |
| Palm orientation | Palms initially facing each other, then rotate outward. |
| Movement | From an "inverted V" starting position above the head, both hands extend downward and outward with **wide amplitude**, mimicking the broad, arched soaring profile and the diamond/wedge tail. |
| Non-manual | Slight head tilt up, eyes follow the upward arc — emphasises soaring posture. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** The wedge-shaped tail and arched-wing soaring
silhouette are the field marks that make this species the most
visually distinctive raptor in Australia.

---

## 2. Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus macropus*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Dominant hand in **point-1** (index finger extended). |
| Location | Starts at forehead level, ends at waist level. |
| Palm orientation | Palm down throughout. |
| Movement | **Single rapid vertical drop**, executed in under one second. The sharp acceleration is the iconic component. |
| Non-manual | Mouth tightens; eyes follow the index finger. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** The vertical hunting stoop, in which the
falcon reaches > 300 km/h — the fastest sustained motion in the
animal kingdom.

---

## 3. Spotted Harrier (*Circus assimilis*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Both hands in **flat-B**. |
| Location | Lateral, at hip level. |
| Palm orientation | Palms down. |
| Movement | **Lateral oscillating glide**, both hands tracing a low, gentle wave from one side to the other, simulating the shallow-V dihedral and ground-skimming flight. |
| Non-manual | Eyes track the hands left-to-right. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** Low quartering flight just above the
vegetation, a behaviour shared with no other Australian raptor.

---

## 4. Brown Goshawk (*Tachyspiza fasciata*, ex *Accipiter fasciatus*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Dominant hand in **curved-5** (slightly bent fingers spread). |
| Location | Front of body, between shoulder and hip. |
| Palm orientation | Palm forward. |
| Movement | **Rapid zigzag**, alternating left-right and up-down, mimicking pursuit through dense forest canopy. |
| Non-manual | Quick alternating eye saccades. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** Maneuvering between trees in pursuit of prey
is the defining flight pattern; the genus reclassification (2024) is
worth noting in any published vocabulary documentation.

---

## 5. Nankeen Kestrel (*Falco cenchroides*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Dominant hand in **flat-5** (open palm, fingers spread). |
| Location | At eye level, in front of the body. |
| Palm orientation | Palm down. |
| Movement | **Stationary vibration** — small rapid oscillations in a fixed location, simulating the hover with continuous wing-beat compensation. |
| Non-manual | Eyes fixed on the hand; slight squint. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** This is the only Australian falcon that
hovers systematically. The stationary signal is the key contrast
with the Peregrine sign (rapid descent).

---

## 6. Black-shouldered Kite (*Elanus axillaris*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Both hands in **H** (index and middle fingers extended, others closed). |
| Location | Chest height, hands ~30 cm apart. |
| Palm orientation | Palms down. |
| Movement | Brief **stationary hover** (~1 s) followed by a **short downward descent** of ~10 cm, simulating the species' characteristic hover-and-pounce hunting strategy. |
| Non-manual | Eyes track the descent. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** Distinguishes from Nankeen Kestrel (also
hovers) by the descent component, which encodes the prey-strike.

---

## 7. Square-tailed Kite (*Lophoictinia isura*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Dominant hand in **flat-B**. |
| Location | Lateral, at chest height. |
| Palm orientation | Palm down. |
| Movement | **Slow horizontal glide** with a subtle wave; the non-dominant hand traces a short horizontal line below the dominant hand to demarcate the **square tail**. |
| Non-manual | Calm pace; eyes track the slow movement. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** The square-tailed silhouette over forest
canopy distinguishes this species from other kites.

---

## 8. Little Eagle (*Hieraaetus morphnoides*)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| Hand shape | Dominant hand in **compact-O** (loose fist, thumb on side). |
| Location | Centred, chest height. |
| Palm orientation | Palm forward. |
| Movement | **Small active loop**, suggesting compactness and the broad-winged silhouette of a small eagle; the loop should be tighter and quicker than the Wedge-tailed sign. |
| Non-manual | Slight nod, conveying smaller scale than larger eagles. |

**Diagnostic anchor:** Small body but classic eagle proportions.
The contrast with Wedge-tailed Eagle's wide-amplitude motion is
intentional and pedagogically useful.

## Open questions for consultants

1. **Hand shape compatibility:** Are any of the proposed hand
   shapes phonologically marked or culturally inappropriate for
   bird-of-prey context in AUSLAN?
2. **Disambiguation:** Are *Falco cenchroides* (kestrel hover) and
   *Elanus axillaris* (kite hover-and-descend) sufficiently
   different to avoid confusion in real-world signing? If not, we
   propose adding a supplementary location parameter (e.g. kestrel
   at eye level, kite at chest level).
3. **Regional variation:** Should we accommodate Northern (NSW/QLD)
   vs. Southern (VIC/TAS/SA) AUSLAN dialect variation? If so, this
   doubles the validation effort.
4. **Naming convention:** Should each sign also encode the
   common-name initialisation (W-T-E for Wedge-tailed Eagle, etc.),
   following the international convention for scientific sign
   vocabularies (Quinto-Pozos & Reynolds, 2012)?
