# Email Template — First Contact

**Recipients (suggested order):**

1. Deaf Society of NSW — `info@deafsocietynsw.org.au`
   (largest Deaf community organisation in NSW; runs cultural and
   educational programs, well-positioned to refer consultants).
2. Royal Institute for Deaf and Blind Children (RIDBC) — partnership
   inquiries via `info@ridbc.org.au` (educational focus, has
   interpreters and AUSLAN linguists on staff).
3. Auslan Signbank team — Trevor Johnston, Macquarie University,
   `trevor.johnston@mq.edu.au` (academic AUSLAN corpus authority,
   strong precedent of engagement with research projects).
4. Deaf Australia — national peak body, `office@deafaustralia.org.au`.

**When to send:** after submitting the MPhil application to UQ but
before the formal admission decision. Mention the application status
in the email — it signals seriousness without making the request
contingent on admission.

---

## Subject

```
Research collaboration: AUSLAN vocabulary for Australian raptors
(MPhil project, University of Queensland)
```

---

## Email body

```
Dear [Title and Name],

My name is Brian Fernández Báez, a biologist applying to a Master
of Philosophy at the University of Queensland. My MPhil project
combines convolutional neural networks for raptor identification
with the development of a specialised AUSLAN vocabulary for the
eight raptor species of southeast Australia. The motivation comes
from my undergraduate thesis in Mexico, where I built a parallel
system using Mexican Sign Language (LSM) for the Veracruz raptor
migration corridor — there are equivalent gaps in scientific
vocabulary in both Australia and Mexico, and Australia is the
project's natural next step given the Black Summer recovery
context.

I am writing because I have prepared a draft of eight proposed
signs (one per species), each derived from the diagnostic flight or
behavioural pattern of the species. These drafts have not been
validated yet — the project commits explicitly to participatory
design with the Deaf community before any sign is published.

I would like to ask whether [organisation name] would be willing
to:

1. Review the eight draft sign descriptions (4-page document
   attached) and suggest revisions for cultural and linguistic
   appropriateness.
2. Refer me to one or two AUSLAN-fluent consultants — ideally one
   Deaf signer and one signer with an interest in natural sciences
   — who could collaborate on the iterative refinement and
   validation phase.
3. Advise on appropriate honoraria and protocol for working with
   Deaf consultants in a research context.

The validation methodology, indicative budget and a draft consent
form are also available on request. Funding for honoraria and
short videographed sign production (~15 seconds per sign) is
included in the project's research budget.

The current prototype web application — built and trained as part
of my undergraduate thesis adaptation — is functional with
provisional motion illustrations clearly labelled as such. I can
share a live demo or a screen recording on request.

Thank you for your time. I am happy to schedule a 20-minute video
call at your convenience to discuss further.

Sincerely,
Brian Fernández Báez
[email]
[phone, optional]

Attachments: sign_descriptions.md (4 pages); project_one_pager.pdf
```

---

## Response handling

- **If they say yes**: send `sign_descriptions.md` and
  `validation_protocol.md` together. Schedule the first video
  call within two weeks.
- **If they redirect**: ask for the most appropriate contact and
  forward the same email with a brief intro line.
- **If they decline or do not respond within 3 weeks**: send a
  polite follow-up. If still no response, move to the next
  organisation in the list.

## After the first call

Keep a written record of every meeting (date, attendees, decisions,
action items). This becomes the audit trail for the participatory
design claim in the final thesis.
